\section{Literature Synthesis and Observations}
\label{sec:literature-synthesis}

\subsection{Operating Context and Traffic-Management Frameworks}
\label{sec:literature-1}

Operating concepts and airspace-design studies for urban air mobility (UAM) relate airspace organization to aircraft capabilities, operating procedures, and coordination responsibilities~\cite{L088,L024}. Traffic-management studies examine how functions such as access control, flight planning, and conflict management interact and affect shared operations~\cite{L028,L041}.

\observation{1}{When a localized portion of the airspace becomes unavailable, the affected flights still share operating rules and resources with the surrounding traffic. The response is therefore a traffic-coordination problem as well as a routing problem.}

\subsection{Airspace Structure and Maneuver Feasibility}
\label{sec:literature-2}

Graph abstractions of urban airspace show how its connectivity changes with width and altitude~\cite{L037}. Corridor-protection and contingency-planning studies relate the space needed for maneuvering to the motion limits of the aircraft~\cite{L036,L020}. Emergency-trajectory planning studies account for corridor limits, protected regions around emergency aircraft, and aircraft turning constraints~\cite{L015}.

\observation{2}{Geometric connectivity of the remaining airspace is not enough to show that an executable response exists. Maneuver feasibility depends jointly on that airspace, aircraft performance, and surrounding traffic.}

\subsection{Traffic Planning and Coordination}
\label{sec:literature-3}

Strategic methods manage planned interactions through flight-level assignment or through the timing of waypoint occupancy~\cite{L013,L017}. Tactical and hybrid approaches handle conflicts through different combinations of preflight planning and in-flight coordination~\cite{L051}. Simulations with limited communication show that coordination performance changes with the traffic information available to the decision-makers~\cite{L053}.

\observation{3}{The applicability of a method after a blockage depends on its decision timing, information requirements, and permitted control actions. These conditions need to be examined for aircraft that are already in flight when the blockage occurs.}

\subsection{Disruption Response and Contingency Management}
\label{sec:literature-4}

Adaptive replanning changes flight paths and timing in response to disturbances in the operating environment~\cite{L012}. Contingency landing management assesses whether a degraded aircraft can still reach a landing site~\cite{L066}. Emergency-trajectory studies plan lower-priority flights around priority traffic and examine how the resulting delays propagate or can be reduced~\cite{L015,L079}. These methods address different initiating events and pursue different response objectives~\cite{L012,L066,L079}.

\observation{4}{The outcome used to define a completed response depends on the event and the purpose of the procedure. Resolving an immediate conflict, completing an emergency landing, and rejoining a route to continue a flight require different completion criteria.}

\subsection{Network Configuration and Resilience}
\label{sec:literature-5}

UAM network design connects facility locations, fleet resources, and operating decisions to the service the network provides~\cite{L087}. Backup vertiports and routes can increase expected throughput during disruptions, but the gains differ across pairs of origins and destinations~\cite{L063}. Verification methods for node closures assess whether landing capacity can accommodate diverted flights under specified routing and travel-time assumptions~\cite{L069}.

\observation{5}{The benefit of redundancy depends on where it is available and on how redirected traffic uses it. Aggregate network performance alone does not establish whether every affected flight has adequate contingency support.}

\subsection{Performance Evaluation, Uncertainty, and Verification}
\label{sec:literature-6}

Simulation studies report throughput, delay, and separation-related outcomes, and level-of-service methods assess aircraft, passenger, and facility performance separately~\cite{L041,L090}. Verification studies establish properties such as landing-capacity compliance or the feasibility of contingency trajectories, but only under the assumptions they specify~\cite{L066,L069}. Probabilistic airspace models relate the required protected space to the uncertainty representation and the selected risk level~\cite{L085}.

\observation{6}{Feasibility, safety-related properties, and service performance are different kinds of claims and call for different evidence. An evaluation needs to state which aircraft and network effects it measures and under what assumptions its conclusions hold.}

\subsection{Disruption Recovery and Joint Design in Related Transportation Systems}
\label{sec:literature-7}

Recovery studies in railway and airline networks relate infrastructure availability and resource reassignment to service continuity and subsequent delays~\cite{L054,L060,L004}. Railway studies have evaluated track layout and dispatching strategy together to quantify their combined effect on corridor operations~\cite{L046}. UAM architecture studies also show that spatial arrangements perform differently under different traffic-management approaches~\cite{L028,L051}.

\observation{7}{The effect of a spatial design change depends on how traffic uses the available airspace under the disruption conditions. Therefore, spatial changes and operating procedures need to be evaluated together when improving a corridor design.}

\section{Research Formulation}
\label{sec:research-formulation}

\subsection{Study Context}
\label{sec:study-context}

This study considers a localized portion of a Cooperative Area (CA) that becomes unavailable for passage at a fixed location. The affected aircraft will be rerouted within the remaining available airspace and coordinated with surrounding traffic. For a rerouted aircraft, the response ends when it rejoins an available route on which its flight can continue.

\subsection{Research Objective}
\label{sec:research-objective}

\begin{quote}
\textit{This study aims to develop an integrated framework for emergency procedure development, performance evaluation, and corridor design improvement in response to localized blockages within a Cooperative Area.}
\end{quote}

\subsection{Research Questions}
\label{sec:research-questions}

\subsubsection{RQ1. Emergency Procedure Development}
\label{sec:rq1}

\begin{quote}
\textit{A localized portion of a Cooperative Area at a fixed location becomes unavailable for passage. How can emergency procedures be designed to reroute and coordinate affected aircraft within the remaining available airspace while satisfying separation and aircraft performance constraints?}
\end{quote}

The work under RQ1 will develop emergency procedures and demonstrate safe and feasible flight for every aircraft represented in a defined case study. The assessment will include surrounding traffic subject to coordination or separation requirements. Efficiency will not be an optimization objective in RQ1. Any conclusion about feasibility will apply only to the conditions and assumptions examined.

\subsubsection{RQ2. Performance Evaluation}
\label{sec:rq2}

\begin{quote}
\textit{How can appropriate evaluation metrics be selected and applied to assess the performance of emergency procedures and corridor designs under localized blockages?}
\end{quote}

The work under RQ2 will select and define metrics for evaluating emergency procedures and corridor designs. The evaluation will include effects on surrounding traffic and the corridor network. These results will provide a basis for comparing performance and identifying where design changes are needed.

\subsubsection{RQ3. Corridor Design Improvement}
\label{sec:rq3}

\begin{quote}
\textit{How can spatial corridor design and operational design be jointly optimized to improve emergency response performance within a Cooperative Area?}
\end{quote}

The work under RQ3 will use the evaluation from RQ2 to optimize the spatial arrangement and operating rules together. Each candidate design must satisfy the safety-related and aircraft performance requirements used in procedure development.
